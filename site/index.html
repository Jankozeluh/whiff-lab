Experiment 1 · Win probability

What are the odds?

Any moment of any game since 1908: the home team's chance to win, how sure that number is, how many real games reached the same spot, and the latest of them. Learned from 15,211,482 plate appearances.

  • The longest odds ever overcome: Chicago (NL) on June 29, 1952, down 8–2, top of the 9th, two outs, bases empty. About 1 in 14,000.
  • Comebacks rise and fall with scoring: across 118 seasons the two move together (correlation 0.76).
  • Since the runner on second, the home team's edge in extra innings has shrunk from 52.4% to 50.0%.
  • A table of nine thousand game states is hard to beat. Smoothing helps where it is thin, and a formula wins when the rules change.

How the lab works

The data is Retrosheet's: every play of 199,897 AL and NL games, typed in by volunteers from scorebooks and newspapers, free for any use with its notice. The lab replays each game and checks that it ends on the official final score (all of them do). The models are built one step at a time, and each step is explained in a lab note in plain words, with what it gains over the last one on a test it never saw.

Everything reruns by itself once a year, after Retrosheet publishes the new season: free tools on a public repository, nothing to pay for and no account. The code and the tables are on GitHub.
