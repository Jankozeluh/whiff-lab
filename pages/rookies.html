The finding

The most important number on a rookie card is the birth date

Of all hitters who were rookies at 20, {{rr.age_curve.hitter.0.rate|pct0}} went on to make at least two All-Star teams in the next nine seasons. At 24 it was {{rr.age_curve.hitter.4.rate|pct0}}, at 27 {{rr.age_curve.hitter.7.rate|pct0}}. Age alone picks future stars better than everything else on the back of the card, and better than the Rookie of the Year voters.

What didn't help

  • Taking the luck out of his rates. A rookie's strikeout, walk and home run rates and his average on balls in play are shrunk toward the league's, so 300 plate appearances can't say more than they know. It makes the luck readable for each rookie, but it predicts stars only a hair better than his raw numbers with his age.
  • His pitches. Since 1988 Retrosheet has every pitch: how often a hitter's swings missed, how often a pitcher made them miss, how often each swung or threw a ball. The card never shows these. Added to the model on the rookies of {{rp.test}}, they changed nothing measurable.
  • A fancier model. Gradient boosting did worse than the plain logistic regression: a few thousand rookies are too few for it.

So the edge is not hidden data. It is reading the data that every card shows the right way: the birth date first, then how much he played, his power and his strikeouts.

How to read it

  • A star means All-Star teams. They are picked by fans, players and the league, and every club gets at least one, so the label is popularity as much as value.
  • A rookie is a player in the season he first goes past 130 at-bats or 50 innings, MLB's limits. A late call-up who stays under them is a rookie the next year.
  • Players who came from Japan's, Korea's or Taiwan's top leagues arrive as grown pros, and the model's age penalty is unfair to them. They are marked.
  • What the model can't see: injuries, minor-league records, scouting reports, how hard he hits the ball. Professional projections use those; the lab only uses free, open data.
  • New classes arrive when the Lahman database publishes a season, usually early in the next year; the report then rebuilds by itself.
